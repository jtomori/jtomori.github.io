**Volumetric video for synthetic training data** [ <- ](..) _2025-05-01_ In this post, I propose an approach I haven't seen before: enhancing the realism of synthetic data for human-centric computer vision models using volumetric video. I believe volumetric video combines the benefits of the real world (photorealism) with those of synthetic data (flexibility and scalability). I'll explain what I mean below. # TL;DR Which of the images below do you find realistic, and which do you not? ![Image 1, [source](https://microsoft.github.io/FaceSynthetics/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/intro_fakeit.jpg) ![Image 2, [source](https://github.com/SelfishGene/SFHQ-T2I-dataset target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/intro_sfhq.jpg) ![Image 3, [source](https://actors-hq.com/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/intro_actorshq_comp.jpg) To many, the second and third images look real, while the first seems off[^fakeit]. The gist of this post is that when it comes to the realism of synthetic training data, volumetric video is superior to 3D avatars. Although the "text to image" result looks real, it isn't very useful for 3D use cases. Unlike a 360° (3 DOF) video - which fixes you in one spot - a volumetric video supports movement through the scene (6 DOF). Volumetric video is captured at multi-camera stages (like the one below with 106 cameras), and the processed output is an animated 3D object. [I could talk about volcap for hours](https://jtomori.github.io/fvv_workshop_2021_aka_public/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"), but I will do my best to keep it brief here :). You can try this [example volumetric video](https://view.dimensionstudio.co/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") in your browser. ![A typical volumetric video stage, [source](https://hhoppe.com/fvv_supplemental.pdf target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/fvv.jpg) ![Example of a volumetric video asset, [source](https://hhoppe.com/proj/fvv/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/fvv.gif) ## Is it any good? It can be. Volumetric video is realistic enough for [Hollywood](https://fmx.de/en/program-2023/detail/event/26744 target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") [productions](https://dimensionstudio.co/work/universal-s-wicked/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") or lightweight enough for [mobile AR experiences](https://dimensionstudio.co/work/burger-king-tinie-tempah-on-a-whopper/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"). Volcap advantages include photorealism, flexibility, and scalability which will come in handy when I will discuss some of the challenges associated with real world and synthetic training data below. ## Challenges of volumetric video Volcap definitely has some challenges, but most can be mitigated: - Baked-in motion: you can't completely alter captured movements, though minor reposing is feasible - Limited resolution: fine details (e.g., loose hair) can be lost if camera resolution is insufficient - this depends on the capture stage - Relightability: realistic relighting requires a specialized lighting setup or extra post-processing - Cost: capture and reconstruction are expensive, though per-asset cost decreases at scale The [What's next for volumetric video](#what'snextforvolumetricvideo) section highlights how academia and industry are tackling these challenges. # What is synthetic data anyway A major hurdle in applying modern machine learning (ML) to complex tasks is the scarcity of labeled data. Synthetic data - artificially generated training data - bridges this gap, effectively replicating real experimental data. Synthetic data is valuable when real data is dangerous, expensive, slow, or impossible to capture. If you're already familiar with synthetic data, skip ahead to the [Volumetric video for synthetic data](#volumetricvideoforsyntheticdata) section. ![Synthetic data example, [source](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/parallel-domain-says-autonomous-driving-wont-scale-without-synthetic-data/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/synth_data.jpg) ## Is it any good? It can be. Synthetic data has advanced research from [autonomous driving](#whatissyntheticdataanyway/interestingcasestudies/alvinn:anautonomouslandvehicleinaneuralnetwork) to [facial landmark detection](#whatissyntheticdataanyway/interestingcasestudies/fakeittillyoumakeit:faceanalysisinthewildusingsyntheticdataalone). With care, it can rival real data and even offer annotations impossible to gather manually. ## Interesting case studies ### ALVINN: An Autonomous Land Vehicle In A Neural Network ![](images/alvinn.jpg) _Dean A. Pomerleau, 1988_, [pdf](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/1988/file/812b4ba287f5ee0bc9d43bbf5bbe87fb-Paper.pdf target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") Describes a neural network (NN) architecture for predicting steering direction based on video and lidar inputs. Here are a couple of excerpts: - _Training has been conducted using simulated road images. Successful tests on the Carnegie Mellon autonomous navigation test vehicle indicate that the network can effectively follow real roads under certain field conditions._ - _Training on actual road images is logistically difficult, because in order to develop a general representation, the network must be presented with a large number of training exemplars depicting roads under a wide variety of conditions. Collection of such a data set would be difficult, and changes in parameters such as camera orientation would require collecting an entirely new set of road images. To avoid these difficulties we have developed a simulated road generator which creates road images to be used as training exemplars for the network._ - _At the relatively low resolution being used it is difficult to distinguish between real and simulated roads._[^alvinn] - _Training involves first creating a set of 1200 road snapshots depicting roads with a wide variety of retinal orientations and positions, under a variety of lighting conditions and with realistic noise levels._ - _Performance of the network to date is comparable to that achieved by the best traditional vision-based autonomous navigation algorithm at CMU under the limited conditions tested._ - _Namely, in this paradigm the data, not the programmer, determines the salient image features crucial to accurate road navigation. From a practical standpoint, this data responsiveness has dramatically sped ALVINN's development. Once a realistic artificial road generator was developed, backpropagation produced in half an hour a relatively successful road following system. It took many months of algorithm development and parameter tuning by the vision and autonomous navigation groups at CMU to reach a similar level of performance using traditional image processing and pattern recognition techniques._ To sum it up, the researcher has found that synthetic data was: good enough for training an NN which performed well when presented with real data, it was easier, more flexible, and faster to generate synthetic data than to capture real data, it has resulted in a comparable performance to traditional autonomous driving approaches and has realized that the quality of training data is crucial to the performance of the NN. [^alvinn]: _I find this observation hilarious_ ### Fake It Till You Make It: Face analysis in the wild using synthetic data alone ![](images/fakeit.jpg) _Erroll Wood, Tadas Baltrušaitis, Charlie Hewitt, Sebastian Dziadzio, Matthew Johnson, Virginia Estellers, Thomas J. Cashman, Jamie Shotton, 2021_, [project page](https://microsoft.github.io/FaceSynthetics/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") This is a significant attempt at settling the synthetic vs real data debate ;) - _We train machine learning systems for face-related tasks such as landmark localization and face parsing, showing that synthetic data can both match real data in accuracy as well as open up new approaches where manual labelling would be impossible._ - _We demonstrate that it is possible to perform face-related computer vision in the wild using synthetic data alone._ - _Researchers have tried to bridge this gap with data mixing, domain adaptation, and domain-adversarial training, but we show that it is possible to synthesize data with minimal domain gap, so that models trained on synthetic data generalize to real in-the-wild datasets._ - _Pixel-perfect segmentation labels let us achieve near-SOTA results using off-the-shelf neural networks._ - _We have shown that it is possible to achieve results comparable with the state of the art for two well-trodden tasks: face parsing and landmark localization, without using a single real image during training._ - _Assuming $1 per hour for an M60 GPU (average price across cloud providers), it would cost $7,200 to render 100,000 images. Though this seems expensive, real data collection costs can run much higher, especially if we take annotation into consideration._ This relatively recent research shows that: highly realistic synthetic data alone can be as good as the real data, it is cheaper to generate, it can contain annotations that would be impossible to label by hand, and shows the flexibility of the dataset, e.g. repurposing it for a VR eye tracking application. An interesting observation is that the increased realism of synthetic data has removed the necessity of domain gap reducing strategies like data mixing or domain adaptation. Similarly, the increased realism of synthetic humans is the main benefit that volcap can bring to the synthetic data in my opinion. ## Advantages of synthetic data Based on the above case studies we can conclude that for some research problems synthetic data: - Can match or exceed real data quality, even when used alone without any real data - Is flexible, cheaper, and faster to produce - E.g. if the sensor configuration changes it is not needed to re-capture all of the real data, the synthetic data just needs to be rerendered with the new parameters - Can produce consistent labels that would be impossible to annotate manually - Can bridge the gap between rare and common-class data by upsampling rare classes in a simulation - E.g. we could add cyclists to our autonomous vehicles synthetic data if they were underrepresented in the real data Some additional advantages are regarding the safety of capturing edge cases. In the context of autonomous vehicles synthetic data would allow us to create a wide range of driving scenarios, including complex, rare, and dangerous situations, without the risks associated with real-world testing like jaywalkers, kids unexpectedly running across a busy street, people on construction sites, around broken vehicles, drivers falling asleep and so on. To be clear, I'm not trying to claim here that synthetic data is a magic solution to all of your research problems. This is not a comprehensive literature review and definitely suffers from the survivorship bias. But I hope that it gets across the point of viability, or even preferability of synthetic data for some use cases. ## Challenges of synthetic data Training with synthetic data, however, does add one new hurdle: because synthetic data often differs from real world data in subtle ways, a domain gap exists between them. Those subtle differences can negatively influence the accuracy of the ML model when presented with real data. This is especially true for synthetic humans, which are notoriously hard to get right. There are multiple approaches for minimizing the domain gap, e.g. - Making the synthetic data more similar to the real data (see this [blog post](https://paralleldomain.com/not-all-synthetic-datasets-are-created-equal target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") for an example) - Training on a mix of synthetic and real data and then fine-tuning on real data only (see this [blog post](https://www.appliedintuition.com/blog/using-synthetic-data-effectively-at-scale target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") for an example) Below, I argue that volumetric video can reduce the domain gap by boosting realism. This follows the [Fake It Till You Make It](#whatissyntheticdataanyway/interestingcasestudies/fakeittillyoumakeit:faceanalysisinthewildusingsyntheticdataalone) observation that increasing the realism of training data enables models to generalize more effectively to real-world data. # Volumetric video for synthetic data Now that I have introduced volcap and synthetic data individually, let's talk about how I think volcap could enhance synthetic data. Identifying and analyzing humans is fundamental to many vision applications. Real world human data is hard (expensive, dangerous, slow) to collect at scale, and synthetic humans are challenging to hand-craft. Volumetric video provides photorealistic 3D captures of humans in motion, combining the best of real and synthetic data: flexibility and scalability of synthetic with the realism of real. **Key benefits of volumetric video assets** - **Flexible:** volcap assets are 3D assets in motion, are compatible with game engines or Visual Effects (VFX) workflows, are compatible with existing synthetic environments - Because of being 3D they are very flexible - they can be viewed from any angle, can be converted to LIDAR samples, and depth maps, used for collisions in simulations - Individual volcap assets can be combined to form large crowds, pedestrians - **Customizable:** assets can be further modified - added props, clothing, changed colours of clothing, and skin tone which provides a lot of flexibility once the asset is captured - This is analogous to the approach taken by [Fake It Till You Make It](#whatissyntheticdataanyway/interestingcasestudies/fakeittillyoumakeit:faceanalysisinthewildusingsyntheticdataalone) which has captured scans of 500 real humans and augmented them to 100,000 synthetic assets - See [this presentation](https://youtu.be/ITbxJeCoaQY target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") for examples of adding variation to existing assets, including with some generative AI tools - **Photoreal:** volcap doesn't suffer from the "uncanny valley" issues that are associated with hand-crafted 3D avatars (more on that [below](#volumetricvideoforsyntheticdata/whynotavatars)) - Since volcap is captured with cameras it is as real as a photograph can be - realism isn't being added by artists, but is inherent in the capture process - This can help with reducing the domain gap of synthetic data, especially when involving virtual humans - I think this is the key benefit of using volcap in synthetic data - **Scalable:** [you can capture hundreds of actors](https://fmx.de/en/program-2023/detail/event/26744 target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") over a few days resulting in thousands of assets with varying actions, appearances - **General:** you can capture almost anything that fits into the capture stage, including animals, complex costumes, [fast motion](https://dimensionstudio.co/work/sky-sports-golf-open-2021/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"), and so on. If the stage size is a limitation, [you can use a treadmill](https://dimensionstudio.co/work/pretty-green-x-three-adwoa-aboah-lfw/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") - **Multispectral:** volcap stages can capture assets in multiple spectra, e.g. visible light and infrared light, providing accurate training data for applications relying on infrared sensors - **Cost efficient:** even though volcap shoots are not cheap, the overall cost can be reduced by the fact that during a single shooting day, it is possible to capture many actors - Thanks to [existing](https://secure.axyz-design.com/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") [libraries](https://renderpeople.com/3d-people/?_product=4d-people target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") of volcap assets this upfront cost can be skipped altogether and assets can be purchased cheaply for testing ![Volumetric video with annotations, [source](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/arcturus-volumetric-video-editing/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/segmented.jpg) ![Volumetrically captured pedestrians, [source](https://www.chaos.com/anima target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/anima.jpg) ## Why not avatars How do avatars compare to volumetric video? By avatars I mean 3D humans which were created with the traditional VFX / games pipeline. That pipeline involves numerous steps like design, modeling, texturing, shading, rigging, animating, character effects, and so on. Production of avatars is slow and expensive: you need a team of experts in different areas, you need to iterate a lot on a single avatar, and the amount of work increases if you are after realistic avatars in motion (you need to worry about muscle simulations, micro movements, realistic animation, hair simulation, cloth simulation, multi-person interaction and so on), which will be important if you worry about the domain gap of your synthetic data. In a nutshell, for the same cost and time, you can get more realistic and more volcap assets than avatar assets. Because of the realism issues associated with avatars (the infamous uncanny valley), avatar assets are more likely to contribute to the domain gap than volcap assets. Currently, avatars seem to be the dominant approach for adding humans to synthetic datasets. Below I show some examples of typical (in terms of realism) synthetic humans found in today's commercial automotive datasets: ![[source](https://www.skyengine.ai/use-cases/synthetic-data-for-next-gen-driver-monitoring-systems target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/avatar_skyengine.jpg) ![[source](https://anyverse.ai/in-cabin-monitoring/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/avatar_anyverse.jpg) ![[source](https://synthesis.ai/applications/driver-monitoring/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/avatar_synthesis.jpg) These assets required a lot of work and they are pretty good, but they still look more like video game characters than real humans. Here's a volumetric video example for comparison: ![[source](https://actors-hq.com/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer")](images/example_actorshq.jpg) I have compared volcap and avatars on the [CVMP 2021 Industry Special Session](https://youtu.be/Nyo9Cez_Re8?t=56 target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") - feel free to watch that to find out more. Tools like [MetaHumans](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/metahuman target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"), [Character Creator](https://www.reallusion.com/character-creator/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") and various "image to image" ML approaches definitely help with the realism and production efficiency of avatars, but they are still lagging behind photographed volcap assets in terms of realism. # How much realism do we really need It's definitely worth asking this question when dealing with any synthetic data: what level of realism does my application actually require? Or at what point does unrealistic data start to negatively affect my application's performance? Throughout this post, I am advocating for photorealistic synthetic humans captured with volumetric video. I believe that this realism can have a significant impact on applications that deal with questions like _"What is this person doing"_, _"How does this person feel"_, _"Is this person disguising itself by wearing a mask"_. Such questions can be important in human-centric applications, e.g., at home robots, healthcare robots, fraud detection systems, or police response training. Volcap has already found use in police response training, an interesting observation from the [V3 training system](https://www.virtra.com/virtra-introduces-breakthrough-in-realistic-3d-humans-for-police-and-military-training-market/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") is that _"avatars fail at de-escalation training and judgmental use-of-force exercises whereas V3 excels at depicting human interactions accurately"_. The company has instead opted for volumetrically captured actors in its VR training product. On the other hand, if your application analyzes security CCTV footage in which humans occupy at most 100 pixels, volumetric video won't make a large difference - at this scale it's hard to distinguish volcap from avatars assets. # What's next for volumetric video Research and industry are advancing volumetric video, addressing challenges [discussed above](#whatisavolumetricvideoanyway/challengesofvolumetricvideo) such as relightability and drivable motion: - [The Relightables: Volumetric Performance Capture of Humans with Realistic Relighting](https://augmentedperception.github.io/therelightables/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"), 2019 - Uses light stages to infer material properties for realistic relighting - [HumanRF: High-Fidelity Neural Radiance Fields for Humans in Motion](https://actors-hq.com/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"), 2023 - Neural radiance fields for long sequences with fine details and a high-quality dataset - [DifFRelight: Diffusion-Based Facial Performance Relighting](https://www.eyelinestudios.com/research/diffrelight.html target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"), 2024 - Combines Gaussian splats and one-light-at-a-time data captured at a light stage for diffusion-based image-to-image relighting of faces in a volumetric video - [GaussianHeads: End-to-End Learning of Drivable Gaussian Head Avatars from Coarse-to-fine Representations](https://vcai.mpi-inf.mpg.de/projects/GaussianHeads/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"), 2024 - Generates highly dynamic and deformable, drivable human faces in real time with the help of Gaussian splats - [Infinite-Realities's Spatial Capture](https://www.ir-ltd.net/ir/4dgs/ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") - Commercial Gaussian splat reconstruction Currently, a lot of research is spent on radiance fields, and Gaussian splats representations which address some challenges of the traditional triangle meshes representation. There is a growing ecosystem around Gaussian splats in the VFX, games pipelines which looks very promising and this representation is already viable in a commercial setting. # Recap I've introduced volumetric video and synthetic data, discussed their strengths and limitations, and proposed how volumetric video can narrow the realism gap for virtual humans in synthetic training sets. # Bonus section -
